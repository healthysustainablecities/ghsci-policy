Overview

GHSCI Policy helps you assess the presence and quality of recommended policies for your city, metropolitan area or jurisdiction, and generate a Policy Indicators Report for the Global Observatory of Healthy and Sustainable Cities' 1000 Cities Challenge.

You can submit your policy evidence in two ways:

Online form

Complete the online audit form

Enter policy evidence directly in the app. No spreadsheet or local software required. Use the New audit form in the app to get started.

Excel upload

Upload a completed .xlsx checklist

Download the policy checklist workbook, complete it offline, then drop the .xlsx file into the app. The report is generated automatically on upload.

Either pathway produces the same scored Policy Indicators Report and downloadable PDF.

GHSCI Policy is for policy-only analysis. If you also need spatial indicators or a combined Policy and Spatial Indicators Report, use the separate offline GHSCI global-indicators tool.

Step-by-step instructions

Step 1 – Define the audit

Enter the details of the city or jurisdiction being assessed. Where possible, the audit should cover the whole metropolitan area or the area that contains the majority of the metropolitan population. If the audit covers only part of a larger area, explain the reason in the study details.

Identify all relevant levels of government. Depending on the policy area, these may include local, metropolitan, regional, state or national government. Record policies from each level separately.

Step 2 – Find relevant policy documents

Identify formally adopted, current policies that apply to the whole or a substantial part of the study area. Relevant documents include strategies, plans, guidelines, standards, regulations, legislation, and infrastructure or land-use policies.

Use the policy indicators and their descriptions to guide your search. Search documents by relevant keywords and synonyms. Contents pages, headings and document-search functions can help locate relevant sections.

Step 3 – Enter policy evidence

For each relevant policy record:

  • Official policy title
  • Responsible government or agency
  • Government level
  • Adoption or publication year
  • Source URL or citation
  • Relevant policy text (quoted or accurately summarised)
  • Page, section, clause or other location reference
  • Additional notes about applicability or interpretation

If the source is not in English, provide an English translation or summary and retain the original source details. Add each relevant policy as a separate entry rather than combining documents. If an indicator is not relevant to the local context, record this and explain why.

Step 4 – Assess policy indicators

Assess whether the policy aim, action or intent aligns with the principles of healthy and sustainable cities. Base the assessment on the policy evidence you have recorded — do not assess a policy solely on its title or general subject matter.

Measurable targets

A measurable target is a quantitative standard, threshold or commitment. It may include a delivery timeframe. Examples include a specified percentage, a distance or travel-time standard, a numerical infrastructure standard, or a mode-share target with a completion date.

General statements such as "promote active transport" or "improve liveability" are not measurable targets unless they include a measurable value or defined threshold.

If a measurable target is present: record the target exactly as stated, assess whether it is an evidence-informed threshold, and cite relevant research or standards. If you are unsure, select Unclear and explain the uncertainty in the notes.

Step 5 – Review and validate

Before generating a report, check that:

  • Policies are formally adopted and current
  • Policies apply to the selected study area
  • The responsible government and government level are correct
  • Policy evidence supports each assessment
  • Source URLs or citations are complete
  • Page, section or clause references are included
  • Measurable targets have been recorded accurately
  • Unclear or non-applicable items include notes
  • A second reviewer has checked the audit where possible

Step 6 – Generate and review your report

Online form path: Submit the completed form. The app creates a report record and processes it automatically.

Excel path: Drop the completed .xlsx checklist file into the upload area. The app detects the file and begins processing automatically.

Either way, the report card will show a Processing status and update to Completed when the PDF is ready. Read the report carefully and check that the city details, audit period, policy findings and local context are correctly represented.

You can update entries and regenerate the report (using the ⚙️ settings or 🔄 regenerate button) until the content is satisfactory.

Step 7 – Finalise and submit

  1. Complete or review the contextualised summary in Report Settings.
  2. Download the final Policy Indicators Report using the 📄 PDF button.
  3. Email the report to info@healthysustainablecities.org for consideration for publication.
An online submission pathway is planned but not yet available. Until then, reports must be downloaded and submitted by email.

Pre-submission checklist

Before finalising the audit, confirm that:

  • The study area and audit period are clearly defined
  • Relevant government levels have been considered
  • Policy documents are official, current and formally adopted where applicable
  • Each policy entry includes sufficient source information
  • The policy evidence relates directly to the indicator being assessed
  • Multiple relevant policies have been recorded separately
  • Measurable targets have not been confused with general aspirations
  • Target text has been copied accurately
  • Evidence-informed threshold assessments include an explanation
  • Unclear and non-applicable items include notes
  • A second reviewer has checked the audit where possible
  • The draft report has been reviewed before download
  • The final report includes an appropriate contextualised summary
